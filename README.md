Causality Assessment of Adverse Drug Reactions

1. Introduction

Causality assessment is the systematic process of evaluating whether a suspected adverse drug reaction (ADR) is related to a particular medicine. It supports patient safety and rational medicine use.

2. Objectives

- To understand ADR causality assessment.
- To study the WHO-UMC and Naranjo assessment methods.
- To classify suspected reactions as Certain, Probable, or Possible.
- To understand the importance of severity and seriousness assessment.

3. Standard Assessment Methods

A. WHO-UMC Method

The WHO-UMC system classifies suspected ADRs using clinical evidence, timing, alternative explanations, and response to withdrawal of the medicine.

- Certain: A plausible time relationship, unlikely alternative causes, and a clinically convincing reaction.
- Probable/Likely: A reasonable time relationship, alternative causes are unlikely, and improvement after withdrawal is clinically reasonable.
- Possible: A reasonable time relationship exists, but disease or other medicines could also explain the reaction.

B. Naranjo Algorithm

The Naranjo algorithm uses a questionnaire and score to assess the likelihood that a medicine caused an adverse reaction.

Typical score categories:

- 9 or above: Definite
- 5–8: Probable
- 1–4: Possible
- 0 or below: Doubtful

The Naranjo categories are not identical to the WHO-UMC categories.

4. Hypothetical Case Studies

Case 1: Probable ADR

A fictional patient develops a dry cough after starting an ACE inhibitor. The cough improves after the medicine is discontinued under medical supervision, and no more likely cause is identified.

Assessment: Probable/Likely, based on the hypothetical evidence.

Case 2: Possible ADR

A fictional patient develops a skin rash after starting an antibiotic. However, a viral infection could also explain the rash, and further clinical information is unavailable.

Assessment: Possible, because an alternative explanation exists.

Case 3: Certain ADR

A hypothetical case has a well-established time relationship, a highly characteristic reaction, no plausible alternative explanation, and strong clinical evidence supporting the medicine-reaction relationship.

Assessment: Certain only if the full WHO-UMC criteria are satisfied. This example illustrates the criteria rather than establishing a real case.

5. Severity and Seriousness

Severity describes the intensity of a reaction, such as mild, moderate, or severe.

Seriousness is a regulatory classification. An ADR may be considered serious if it results in death, is life-threatening, requires or prolongs hospitalization, causes significant disability, causes a congenital anomaly, or is another medically important event.

Severity and seriousness are related but are not interchangeable.

6. Importance of Causality Assessment

- Supports safer prescribing and medicine use.
- Helps identify potential drug-related risks.
- Improves pharmacovigilance documentation.
- Supports clinical decisions and patient counselling.
- Contributes to medicine safety monitoring.

7. Conclusion

Causality assessment helps evaluate suspected relationships between medicines and adverse reactions. WHO-UMC and the Naranjo algorithm provide structured approaches, but conclusions depend on the quality and completeness of clinical information.
